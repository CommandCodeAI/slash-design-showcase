I The long night

We built a world that cannot forget us.

Every year the industry buries, burns, or ships away enough cloth to bury a city. It does not rot. It leaches. It arrives on a beach eight thousand kilometres from the shop that sold it, and it is still there when the child who wore it is old.

Luxury, as we inherited it, is a promise kept to the buyer and broken to the ground. We wanted to know whether a beautiful thing could be made without leaving a debt behind it.

II The long walk
FieldAtelierReturn

Stage one

The field remembers

We grow flax and hemp on land that was tired. No irrigation, no synthetic nitrogen — only rotation, compost, and patience. Four seasons in, the soil holds water again, and it holds carbon: 4.1 tonnes to the hectare, measured and independently verified.

Latitude 41°S — Te Tauihu

Stage two

The water goes home

Our dyes are grown, not mixed — madder root, walnut hull, indigo, iron. Every litre we draw is filtered through reed beds and returned to the same catchment, clean enough to drink. Nothing leaves the building that did not arrive as a plant.

94% of process water reclaimed

Stage three

The coat comes back

Wear it for a decade. We will mend it for free, twice. When it is finally finished, send it home: we take the buttons off, and the cloth goes into the compost row that feeds next season's flax. A garment becomes a field becomes a garment.

Zero to landfill — 18,400 pieces returned
