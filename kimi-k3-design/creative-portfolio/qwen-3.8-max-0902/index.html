I build interfaces that behave like physical things — weight, momentum, response. Eight years between design and engineering, obsessed with the frames in between: the easing curve, the masked line, the hover that answers back.

I work independently with studios, agencies and product teams — from brand sites and launch moments to design systems that survive real roadmaps. The stack changes; the standard doesn't: 60fps or it doesn't ship, accessible by default, and no detail too small to argue about.

Before going solo I led frontend at a Lisbon product studio and spent two seasons inside an agency machine, learning that the best work happens when the person drawing the curve is the person shipping it.

(03 — Approach)

How the work gets made

01

Design engineering

I erase the seam between design and code. Prototypes live in the browser from day one, so decisions get made in the real material — real type, real motion, real latency.

Figma → Code, same week

02

Motion with meaning

Animation answers questions before they're asked: where did this come from, what just changed, what can I touch. Easing curves carry intent — nothing moves for attention alone.

Transform & opacity only

03

Performance is craft

A 16.7ms frame budget is a design constraint like any other. Compositor-only animation, lazy everything, Core Web Vitals in the green — or it doesn't ship.

98+ median Lighthouse

04

Details compound

Kerning, focus rings, empty states, the three-hundredth hover. Craft is what remains when you care about the parts nobody consciously sees — they add up to the feeling.

Accessible by default
