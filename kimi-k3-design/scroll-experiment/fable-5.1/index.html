Chapter one

The beauty industry produces 120 billion units of packaging a year. Almost none of it goes home.

A cream is used in ninety days. Its jar lasts four centuries. The plastics, the microbeads, the air-freighted water — they don't disappear when you close the lid. They travel: into rivers, into fish, into the soil that grows the next harvest.

We looked at that cycle and asked a simple question. What if a product could leave nothing behind but the forest it came from?

Chapter two

First, we stopped shipping water.

Most creams are 70% water. Ours arrive as a concentrate, waking up with a few drops from your own tap. One shipment does the work of four.

Then we went back to the forest.

Every botanical is wild-harvested at night, when oils are densest, by cooperatives who own the land they walk. We buy the forest's yield, never the forest.

And we designed the jar to disappear.

Pressed mycelium, sealed with beeswax. Bury it, and in ninety days — the same time it takes to finish the cream — it becomes soil again.

Chapter three

Three rituals. One closed loop.

Each product is grown, used and composted inside the same ninety-day cycle. Nothing enters the loop that can't leave it as nourishment.
